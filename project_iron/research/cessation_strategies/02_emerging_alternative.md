## 1. Psychedelic-Assisted Therapy: The "Reset" Switch

### 1.1 The Johns Hopkins Protocol (Psilocybin)
In a landmark pilot study (Johnson et al.), psilocybin-assisted therapy achieved a **67% abstinence rate at 12 months**. Standard treatments (Varenicline) typically achieve ~30%.

#### The Mechanism: "Hyper-Plasticity"
*   **Default Mode Network (DMN):** In addicts, the brain's DMN (responsible for the sense of "self" and rigid habit loops) is overactive and rigid.
*   **The Disruption:** Psilocybin temporarily disintegrates the DMN, allowing the brain to communicate in novel ways. It creates a window of **hyper-plasticity** where deep-seated beliefs ("I am a smoker") can be rewritten.
*   **The Experience:** Patients often report a "Mystical Experience" where they feel a profound sense of unity and a realization that their addiction is "petty" or "irrelevant" to their true self.

#### The 15-Week Clinical Protocol
It is not "taking mushrooms and hoping." It is rigorous therapy.
1.  **Preparation (Weeks 1-4):** 4-6 hours of CBT. Building rapport. Identifying "Why" the patient smokes.
2.  **Session 1 (Week 5):**
    *   *Dose:* Moderate (20mg/70kg).
    *   *Setting:* Living-room-like clinical room. Eye shades, headphones with music playlist. Two therapists present (Male/Female).
    *   *Goal:* Internal exploration.
3.  **Integration (Weeks 6-8):** Discussing the insights. "How does this change your daily life?"
4.  **Session 2 (Week 9):**
    *   *Dose:* High (30mg/70kg).
    *   *Goal:* The breakthrough experience.
5.  **Long-Term Integration:** Applying the new "Identity" to the old environment.

## 2. Clinical Hypnotherapy: Reprogramming the Subconscious

### 2.1 The Theory: Bypassing the "Critical Faculty"
The conscious mind (Critical Faculty) rejects new ideas ("You should quit"). Hypnosis aims to induce a "Theta State" (deep relaxation) where the critical faculty is bypassed, and the subconscious is suggestible.

### 2.2 The "Spiegel Method" (Dr. David Spiegel, Stanford)
A focused, rapid induction technique used clinically.
*   **The 3-Point Reframing Script:**
    1.  **"For my body, smoking is a poison."** (Not a reward).
    2.  **"I need my body to live."** (Survival instinct).
    3.  **"I owe my body respect and protection."** (Stewardship).
*   *Key Difference:* It does not focus on "fighting" the urge (which acknowledges the urge's power). It focuses on *protecting the body*, which makes the urge irrelevant.

### 2.3 Self-Hypnosis (Reveri App)
*   **Tool:** The **Reveri** app (developed by Dr. Spiegel) guides users through this self-hypnosis protocol in <10 mins.
*   **Efficacy:** Research is mixed (high variability), but responsive subjects (high hypnotic susceptibility) show quit rates of **20-40%**.

## 3. Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS): The Hardware Fix

### 3.1 What is it?
TMS uses magnetic fields to stimulate nerve cells in the brain. It is non-invasive (a coil placed on the scalp).
*   **FDA Status:** In 2020, the FDA approved the **Deep TMS H4 Coil** specifically for smoking cessation.

### 3.2 The Target: The Insula & PFC
*   **The Target:** The **Insula** (involved in craving/urge intensity) and the **Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)** (involved in impulse control).
*   **The Protocol:**
    *   *Frequency:* Daily sessions (approx. 20 mins) for 3-6 weeks.
    *   *Action:* High-frequency stimulation to "wake up" the PFC (improving willpower) and "quiet down" the Insula (reducing craving).
*   **Efficacy:** Multicenter trials showed a **28% continuous abstinence rate** at 4 weeks (significant vs sham).
*   **Candidate:** Ideal for those who have failed chemical interventions (NRT/Chantix) and want a direct neurological intervention.

## 4. Behavioral Economics: Deposit Contracts ("Put Your Money Where Your Mouth Is")

### 4.1 The Theory: Loss Aversion
Kahneman & Tversky (Nobel Prize) proved humans feel the pain of a loss **2x more intensely** than the pleasure of a gain.
*   *Traditional Approach:* "If I quit, I save money." (Gain = Weak motivator).
*   *Deposit Contract:* "If I smoke, I lose \$1,000." (Loss = Strong motivator).

### 4.2 The "Stickk" Protocol
Based on research from Yale University.
1.  **The Commitment:** You sign a contract to quit by Date X.
2.  **The Stakes:** You authorize a credit card charge of \$500 (or significant amount).
3.  **The Referee:** You appoint a third party to verify your status (e.g., via urine test strip video).
4.  **The Recipient:**
    *   *Charity:* Nice, but weak ("At least it goes to a good cause").
    *   *Anti-Charity:* The money goes to an organization you **despise** (e.g., a rival political party, a pro-gun/anti-gun lobby depending on your views).
    *   *Why:* The thought of *funding the enemy* creates a visceral disgust towards the cigarette.

### 4.3 Efficacy Data (NEJM)
A study in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (Halpern et al.) found that deposit contracts ($800 risk) resulted in the **highest abstinence rates** of any incentive group tested.
